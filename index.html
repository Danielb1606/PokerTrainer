Preflop

Reading the charts

  • Pairs run down the diagonal. Suited hands sit above it, offsuit hands below.
  • Solid colour is the core range. A lighter shade marks an edge hand: close to break-even, so either action is fine.
  • Percentages count combos out of 1,326.
  • Play edge hands: tight tables where opens often take the blinds, when players behind rarely 3-bet, and in low-rake games.
  • Drop edge hands: loose tables with lots of callers, against frequent 3-bettors, and in high-rake live games.

Facing 3-bets

  • In position you can call wider. Out of position, 4-bet or fold more.
  • Against tight 3-bettors (mostly QQ+ and AK), folding more than the chart is fine.
  • Against aggressive 3-bettors, continue with roughly a third or more of your opens, or they profit by 3-betting any two cards.

Loose-passive live tables

  • Open 3–5bb to match the table.
  • Isolate limpers at 4bb + 1bb per limper, roughly your first-in range minus the weakest offsuit hands.
  • Cut 3-bet bluffs. Keep value 3-bets.
  • Value bet thinner and bigger; bluff less.

Postflop

Board texture checklist

  1. Height: A/K-high, middling (Q–8) or low (7 or less)?
  2. Paired? Paired boards rarely help either player.
  3. Suits: rainbow, two-tone or monotone?
  4. Connectedness: dry, some straight draws, or very connected?
  5. Top pairs: who has more top pairs and overpairs? (range advantage)
  6. Strong hands: who has more two pair or better? (nut advantage)
  7. Plan: small bet often, big bet, or check often.

Small bets vs big bets

You have…Plan
More top pairs and overpairs, strong hands even or betterSmall bet (~33%), often, with almost your whole range
At least as many strong hands, and lots of draws on the boardBig bet (~75%) with strong hands and good draws; check the rest
BB matches your strong hands and has more drawsCheck often; bet big only with strong hands and good draws

Small bets need a bluff to work ~25% of the time and give BB ~20% odds; big bets need ~43% and give ~30%.

Outs × 2 ≈ equity with one card to come; outs × 4 ≈ equity from flop to river (runs high above ~9 outs). With deep stacks, implied odds can justify calling slightly worse prices with draws to the nuts.

Choosing bluffs

  • On the river, bluff with your worst hands first: the ones that can never win at showdown. Blockers only break ties.
  • Ace-high and K-high often beat BB's busted draws on the river. Check them and take the showdown.
  • On the flop and turn, prefer semi-bluffs: draws still win when called.
  • Bluff when the board or turn card favours your range, and against players who fold.
  • Bigger bets allow more bluffs: about 1 bluff per 2 value hands at pot size, 1 per 3 at half pot.
  • Against calling stations, stop bluffing and bet your value hands bigger.
